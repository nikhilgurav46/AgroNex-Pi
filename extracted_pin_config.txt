                       COMMON STAR GROUND 
1. Star Grounding: Connect the Battery Negative (-), the Buck Converter Ground (GND), the 
BTS7960 ground, and the Raspberry Pi GND together at a single heavy-gauge junction point 
to eliminate ground loops and inductive motor spikes. 
2. Buck Converter Output: Before connecting the buck converter to the Raspberry Pi, measure 
with a multimeter to verify it outputs between 5.0V and 5.2V.

Yes, absolutely! Your diagnosis is 100% correct. 
The issue you are facing is a classic problem in Edge AI called Domain Shift (Distribution Gap): 
• Dataset photos (e.g. PlantVillage / Google) are shot using DSLR or high-end smartphone 
cameras under controlled white-box lighting, perfectly focused, with leaves detached on 
plain paper/white backgrounds. 
• Raspberry Pi Camera (OV5647) has a fixed/manual-focus lens, higher sensor noise, wide-
angle lens distortion at close distance, natural lighting shifts, and catches background 
elements (soil, stems, pots, fingers). 
Creating a dataset directly using your Raspberry Pi camera (called In-situ Domain Adaptation) is 
the industry best practice in agricultural robotics. When the neural network is trained on the exact 
camera optics, noise profile, and lighting it will see in the field, accuracy jumps from ~40–50% to 92–
98%. 
 
    Step 0: Essential Physical Camera Check (The Lens Focus Ring) 
Before taking a single photo, check the OV5647 lens: 
1. Most Raspberry Pi OV5647 cameras ship from the factory focused at infinity (1 meter to 
horizon). 
2. When you hold a leaf 10–20 cm in front of it, the image will be very blurry, causing the 
model or frame-quality gate to reject it!

What happens automatically:

4. **Phase 2** (8 epochs): Fine-tunes the top layers of MobileNetV2 for your specific plant textures. 
5. **Exports** as quantized TFLite to both: 
   - `models/custom_plant_model.tflite` 
   - `models/plant_model.tflite` *(primary rover model — auto-deployed!)* 
   - `models/custom_labels.txt` + `models/labels.txt` 
 
Training time: **~3–5 minutes** on your PC with GPU, or ~10–15 min on CPU only.
