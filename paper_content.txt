[P6|Unknown] AbstractΓÇöSmall-scale farms and home gardens lack the labor and expertise to water, mow, and inspect crops on a regular schedule, while plant diseases continue to destroy a significant share of global yield. This paper presents AgroNex-Pi, a low-cost autonomous rover built on the Raspberry Pi 4 that combines five subsystems on one chassis: hysteresis-controlled soil-moisture irrigation, scheduled grass trimming, GPS and compass waypoint navigation, edge-AI plant disease detection using a TensorFlow Lite MobileNetV2 classifier, and live cloud telemetry through a serverless AWS stack. The disease pipeline uses a switchable dual-model design (a five-class custom Tulsi model and an eight-class PlantVillage model) and suppresses false alarms through an image-quality gate, a green-pixel check, a 70% confidence threshold, and 3├ù3 segment majority voting. A multi-threaded Python supervisor built around a finite state machine (FSM) coordinates all tasks, and hardware interlocks prevent the pump, cutter, and drive motors from drawing current at the same time. Experimental results show [INSERT RESULTS: classification accuracy, irrigation response, navigation error, inference latency], indicating that the platform is suitable for small farms, gardens, and nurseries.

[P8|Heading 1] I. INTRODUCTION
[P9|Unknown] Agriculture still underpins the economies of many developing nations, yet it faces labor shortages, water scarcity, and fast-spreading plant disease. The Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) estimates that plant pathogens cause crop losses of roughly 10ΓÇô40% of global production [1]. Early detection is critical, but it normally requires an agronomist to walk the field regularly, which small-scale farmers cannot afford.
[P10|Unknown] Two developments make an affordable alternative possible. First, lightweight networks such as MobileNetV2 [2], together with the TensorFlow Lite runtime [7], allow neural-network inference on single-board computers like the Raspberry Pi 4 [8] within a watt-level power budget. Second, serverless cloud platforms allow remote monitoring without maintaining a dedicated server.
[P11|Unknown] This work presents AgroNex-Pi, an autonomous rover that integrates soil sensing, automated irrigation, grass trimming, GPS-guided navigation, and AI-based disease detection in one embedded platform. Tulsi (Holy Basil, Ocimum tenuiflorum), a plant of cultural and medicinal importance in South Asia, is the primary disease-detection target, and a second model covers broader PlantVillage crops [4].

[P13|Unknown] The principal contributions of this work are:
[P14|Unknown] 1) A fully integrated autonomous agricultural rover combining five subsystems on a single Raspberry Pi 4.

[P16|Unknown] 3) A false-positive suppression chain: quality gate, green-pixel check, confidence gate, and 3├ù3 majority voting that requires more than 50% of segments to be diseased.

[P25|Heading 1] III. SYSTEM DESIGN AND ARCHITECTURE
[P26|Heading 2] A. Hardware Platform
[P27|Unknown] The rover is built around a Raspberry Pi 4 Model B (4 GB, quad-core Cortex-A72 at 1.5 GHz). Analog sensors are digitized by a 16-bit ADS1115 I2C ADC, with a 10-bit MCP3008 SPI ADC as an optional fallback. Two BTS7960 43 A H-bridges drive four 12 V geared motors (left and right pairs in parallel) using 1 kHz software PWM. A 12 V 7 Ah battery powers the system, and an LM2596/XL4016 buck converter supplies 5 V to the Pi. Table II lists the main components.

[P38|Heading 2] C. Software Architecture and Finite State Machine
[P39|Unknown] The software is layered across nine Python modules. Only hardware.py (the hardware abstraction layer) touches GPIO, SPI, I2C, or the camera; controllers (irrigation.py, cutter.py, navigation.py, disease_detect.py) receive hardware objects by dependency injection. Other modules provide persistence (database.py, SQLite), the REST API (app.py, Flask), cloud relay (aws_relay.py), the disease knowledge base (disease_db.py), and centralized constants (config.py).
[P40|Unknown] The supervisor (main.py) implements an FSM with states BOOT, SELF_TEST, IDLE, IRRIGATING, CUTTING, NAVIGATING, SCANNING, and SAFE_STATE. A failed self-test sends the system to SAFE_STATE, where all actuators are de-energized and only monitoring remains active. Four threads run concurrently: a 3-second control loop, the disease scanner, the Flask server (port 5000), and a watchdog that logs a WATCHDOG_TIMEOUT alert if the control-loop heartbeat is stale for more than 30 s. SIGTERM and SIGINT call an emergency stop that de-energizes all relays and motors before the threads are joined.

[P43|Heading 1] IV. SUBSYSTEM IMPLEMENTATION
[P44|Heading 2] A. Soil Moisture Sensing and Automated Irrigation
[P45|Unknown] A capacitive soil sensor on ADS1115 channel A0 is linearized by two-point calibration, with ADS_SOIL_DRY = 21000 (0%, dry air) and ADS_SOIL_WET = 9500 (100%, submerged):
[P46|Unknown] 	M = 100 ├ù (ADS_DRY ΓêÆ raw) / (ADS_DRY ΓêÆ ADS_WET)	(1)
[P47|Unknown] Implausible readings are rejected and the cycle is skipped. A hysteresis band governs the pump: it starts when M < 35%, and stops when M ΓëÑ 65% or after the 60 s safety timeout. A 120 s cooldown protects relay contacts and the pump, and the pump is blocked when the reservoir level falls below 20%. The chassis is stopped before the pump is energized, and every cycle (start/end time, duration, start/end moisture, trigger) is logged to SQLite.
[P48|Heading 2] B. Grass Trimming
[P49|Unknown] The cutter runs for 30 s every 3600 s. It refuses to start if disabled in configuration or if the pump is active, motors are hard-stopped before the relay is energized to avoid voltage sag on the shared 12 V bus, and the stop command is re-applied on every control-loop tick while cutting. An emergency stop de-energizes the relay immediately.

[P55|Heading 2] D. Plant Disease Detection
[P56|Unknown] Detection runs on demand (dashboard or API) or automatically when at least 15% of the frame is green in HSV space (H 35ΓÇô90, S 40ΓÇô255, V 40ΓÇô255). The pipeline is:
[P57|Unknown] 1) Quality gate: frames with Laplacian variance below 12.0 (blur) or mean luminance outside 15ΓÇô250 are rejected with RETAKE_NEEDED, which is not a disease alert.
[P58|Unknown] 2) Segmentation: the 224├ù224 frame (float32, normalized to [0,1]) is divided into a 3├ù3 grid and each segment is classified independently by the TFLite model.
[P59|Unknown] 3) Voting and confidence: a disease alert is raised only if more than 4 of 9 segments are diseased, each with softmax confidence ΓëÑ 70%; otherwise the status is HEALTHY or LOW_CONFIDENCE.
[P60|Unknown] 4) Enrichment and alert: the label is matched in the knowledge base for common name, cause, symptoms, and treatment; the buzzer and LED are triggered; and the annotated JPEG and result are stored in SQLite and uploaded to the cloud.

[P63|Heading 2] E. Dual-Model Architecture
[P64|Unknown] The environment variable AGRONEX_MODEL_PROFILE selects a model at startup: custom (five Tulsi classes: Healthy, Infected, Infested, Wilting, and a background class) or plantvillage (eight classes: Tomato early/late blight and healthy, Potato early/late blight and healthy, Bell Pepper bacterial spot and healthy). The TFLite runtime is loaded through a three-level fallback: tflite_runtime, ai_edge_litert, then full tensorflow.lite.

[P73|Heading 1] VI. CLOUD ARCHITECTURE AND REMOTE MONITORING
[P74|Unknown] The Pi runs a relay thread that, every 5 s, POSTs telemetry JSON to an AWS API Gateway (ap-south-1) endpoint. A Python 3.11 Lambda function writes the payload to an S3 bucket as state.json. Disease-scan JPEGs (base64) are posted after each inference, and a camera snapshot is uploaded every 60 s. In the reverse direction the Pi polls a /command endpoint every 5 s to receive remote commands (e.g., pump_on) stored by Lambda as command.json. The S3-hosted single-page dashboard fetches state.json every 3 s and provides live tiles, a disease tab with confidence bar and treatment advice, a scan gallery, a Leaflet.js navigation map, a disease library, and manual controls. If internet access is lost, all functions remain available through the local Flask dashboard.
